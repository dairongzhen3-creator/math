## F145. Rokhlin’s multiple-mixing problem.

Discipline: Dynamical systems and ergodic theory

Proves that every invertible mixing probability-preserving transformation is mixing of all finite orders, resolving Rokhlin's multiple-mixing problem for a single transformation. Correlations among any finite collection of measurable sets converge to the product of their measures whenever all pairwise time separations diverge. ([Lean](lean/docs/145.md))

## F146. Positive metric entropy for the standard map.

Discipline: Dynamical systems and ergodic theory

Proves that the standard sine map on the two-dimensional torus has positive metric entropy with respect to area for every sufficiently large positive parameter. This establishes Sinai's positive-parameter-measure conjecture for the original family, with the stronger conclusion of a full parameter tail. ([Lean](lean/docs/146.md))

## F147. The near-boundary Birkhoff conjecture.

Discipline: Dynamical systems and ergodic theory

Resolves the near-boundary Birkhoff conjecture for smooth strictly convex planar billiards of positive curvature. Such a billiard is an ellipse whenever a full grazing annulus is continuously foliated by individually invariant essential curves. A continuous physical collar of smooth closed convex caustics also suffices.

## F148. The entropy-rate dimension formula for self-similar measures.

Discipline: Dynamical systems and ergodic theory

For every self-similar measure on the line generated by finitely many contracting similarities, proves $`\dim_{\mathrm H}\mu=\min\{1,h_{\mathrm{RW}}/\chi\}`$, where $`h_{\mathrm{RW}}`$ is the entropy rate of random composed maps and <i>χ</i> the average logarithmic contraction. This resolves the entropy-rate dimension conjecture without a separation assumption, allowing exact overlaps and unequal contraction ratios. ([Lean](lean/docs/148.md))

## F149. Classwise permanence for weakly reversible mass-action systems.

Discipline: Dynamical systems and ergodic theory

Proves the permanence conjecture for every finite weakly reversible mass-action system with fixed positive rate constants. Every positive stoichiometric compatibility class, even an unbounded one, has a common compact convex forward-invariant absorbing set. All positive trajectories in that class therefore eventually share positive lower and finite upper concentration bounds. ([Lean](lean/docs/149.md))

## F150. Weak mixing of triangular billiards with an irrational angle.

Discipline: Dynamical systems and ergodic theory

Proves that the billiard flow in every nondegenerate Euclidean triangle with at least one angle irrational relative to <i>π</i> is weakly mixing for normalized area times uniform direction. This strengthens ergodicity on the entire irrational-angle class, with no genericity or Diophantine restrictions. ([Lean](lean/docs/150.md))

## F151. A C1 counterexample to the entropy conjecture.

Discipline: Dynamical systems and ergodic theory

Constructs a noninvertible <i>C</i><sup>1</sup> self-map of a compact smooth manifold with zero topological entropy but eigenvalue 2 on second homology. This disproves the homological entropy lower bound for general <i>C</i><sup>1</sup> self-maps: homological growth need not force positive orbit complexity. ([Lean](lean/docs/151.md))

## F153. Arithmetic classification and non-Pisot singularity for Bernoulli convolutions.

Discipline: Dynamical systems and ergodic theory

Classifies singular and absolutely continuous unbiased Bernoulli convolutions for every $`\lambda\in(0,1)`$ by an infinite, one-sided approximation condition using explicit finite sets of algebraic units. ## F154. Pointwise multiple ergodic averages for mixing transformations.

Discipline: Dynamical systems and ergodic theory

Proves almost-everywhere convergence of consecutive multiple ergodic averages of every finite length for invertible mixing probability-preserving transformations. For each fixed tuple of bounded functions, the limit is the product of their integrals, along all positive averaging lengths. No mixing rate or standardness assumption on the probability space is required.

## F157. Graph coloring, clique minors, and Colin de Verdière invariants.

Discipline: Combinatorics

Disproves Hadwiger's conjecture even for fractional coloring: arbitrarily large finite simple graphs with independence number at most two satisfy $`\chi_f(G)\gt h(G)`$, where $`h(G)`$ is the largest clique-minor order. Also disproves the fractional Colin de Verdière chromatic bound $`\chi_f(G)\le\mu(G)+1`$. In the positive direction, every finite nonempty graph satisfies $`\chi_{\mathrm{list}}(G)\le C h(G)`$ for a universal constant <i>C</i>. ([Lean](lean/docs/157.md))

## F158. The Euclidean plane cannot be colored with five colors.

Discipline: Combinatorics

Proves that every five-coloring of the Euclidean plane has a monochromatic pair at distance one, with no restriction on the color classes. This advances the Hadwiger–Nelson problem: together with the classical seven-coloring, only six and seven remain possible chromatic numbers of the plane. ([Lean](lean/docs/158.md))

## F159. Erdős’s reciprocal-sum conjecture and quasipolynomial Szemerédi bounds.

Discipline: Combinatorics

Proves Erdős's conjecture that every set of positive integers with divergent reciprocal sum contains arithmetic progressions of every finite length. Quantitatively, for each fixed <i>k</i> ≥ 3, every subset of $`\{1,\ldots,N\}`$ with no nonconstant <i>k</i>-term progression has size at most $`C_kN\exp[-c_k(\log N)^{\varepsilon_k}]`$, with positive constants depending only on <i>k</i>. ([Lean](lean/docs/159.md))

## F160. Superexponential van der Waerden numbers.

Discipline: Combinatorics

Resolves Erdős's superexponential-growth question for van der Waerden numbers. If $`W_r(k)`$ is the least interval length forcing a monochromatic <i>k</i>-term progression in every <i>r</i>-coloring, then $`W_r(k)\gt k^{ck\lfloor\log_2 r\rfloor}`$ for an absolute <i>c</i> &gt; 0, all <i>r</i> ≥ 2 and sufficiently large <i>k</i>, uniformly in <i>r</i>. In particular, $`W_r(k)^{1/k}\to\infty`$ for each fixed <i>r</i>. ([Lean](lean/docs/160.md))

## F161. Counterexamples to Sidorenko’s conjecture and the forcing conjecture.

Discipline: Combinatorics

Disproves Sidorenko's conjecture with a connected bipartite pattern on 35 vertices and 66 edges that occurs less frequently than in a random graph of the same edge density. The same pattern disproves the forcing conjecture of Skokan and Thoma: matching its density and the edge density of a constant graphon need not force quasirandomness. ([Lean](lean/docs/161.md))

## F165. The Harary–Hill and Zarankiewicz crossing-number formulas.
