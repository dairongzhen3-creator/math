## F186. Uniform influence and sharp thresholds for graph and hypergraph properties.

Discipline: Combinatorics

Proves the Friedgut–Kalai threshold-width conjectures for graphs and fixed-uniformity hypergraphs. For fixed $`0\lt \varepsilon\lt 1/2`$, every nontrivial increasing relabeling-invariant property crosses from probability <i>ε</i> to $`1-\varepsilon`$ within width $`O((\log n)^{-2})`$ for graphs and $`O_r((\log n)^{-r/(r-1)})`$ for <i>r</i>-uniform hypergraphs, <i>r</i> ≥ 3. The hypergraph influence bound also applies to nonmonotone properties. ([Lean](lean/docs/186.md))

## F187. Snaky in 21 Maker moves.

Discipline: Combinatorics

Settles the Snaky achievement problem: Maker can force the six-cell Snaky shape within 21 of its own moves on the initially empty infinite square board. Maker moves first, each player claims one free cell per turn, and translations, rotations and reflections count as wins. ([Lean](lean/docs/187.md))

## F188. The sharp terminal leave in random triangle removal.

Discipline: Combinatorics

Starting from the complete graph on <i>n</i> vertices, repeatedly delete a uniformly chosen remaining triangle. The terminal edge count is asymptotic to $`n^{3/2}/(2\sqrt2)`$, with mean-square convergence after normalization by <i>n</i><sup>3/2</sup>. This proves the triangle case of the Joos–Kühn sharp-constant conjecture. ([Lean](lean/docs/188.md))

## F189. Cycle–clique Ramsey numbers.

Discipline: Combinatorics

Proves the Erdős–Faudree–Rousseau–Schelp conjecture: $`R(C_m,K_n)=(m-1)(n-1)+1`$ for every $`m\ge n\ge3`$, except $`R(C_3,K_3)=6`$. This is the exact threshold forcing a red <i>m</i>-cycle or a blue <i>n</i>-clique in every red–blue coloring of a complete graph. ([Lean](lean/docs/189.md))

## F190. Polynomial removal fails for ordered binary matrices.

Discipline: Combinatorics

Disproves polynomial ordered binary matrix removal with one fixed $`66\times66`$ zero–one pattern. Matrices can require many binary-entry changes to become pattern-free while their copy density is smaller than every proposed polynomial bound in that distance. Copies preserve row and column orders and match both zeros and ones. ([Lean](lean/docs/190.md))

## F191. A power improvement in the Heilbronn triangle lower bound.

Discipline: Combinatorics

For every sufficiently large <i>n</i>, constructs <i>n</i> points in the unit square such that every triangle has area at least $`n^{-2+c}`$ for one absolute <i>c</i> &gt; 0. This disproves the conjectured almost-<i>n</i><sup>−2</sup> upper bound in Heilbronn's triangle problem, which asks how large the smallest determined triangle can be. ([Lean](lean/docs/191.md))

## F192. Boolean functions violate the square-root degree bound by arbitrary factors.

Discipline: Combinatorics

Disproves the proposed square-root bound relating a Boolean function's linear Fourier coefficients to its polynomial degree. For every <i>C</i> &gt; 0, there is a sign-valued Boolean function <i>f</i> with $`\sum_i\widehat f(\{i\})\gt C\sqrt{\deg(f)}`$. Thus its total signed correlation with individual input bits can exceed the proposed bound by an arbitrary factor. ([Lean](lean/docs/192.md))

## F193. Serre’s intersection-multiplicity conjecture.

Discipline: Algebra

Proves strict positivity of Serre's intersection multiplicity $`\chi^R(M,N)`$ for nonzero finitely generated modules over any regular local ring, provided $`M\otimes_R N`$ has finite length and $`\dim M+\dim N=\dim R`$. This resolves the positivity conjecture, including ramified mixed characteristic.

## F197. A torsion-free group algebra that is not directly finite.

Another counterexample is an integral group-ring matrix, invertible over the rational group ring, with Fuglede–Kadison determinant strictly between zero and one, disproving the unrestricted Determinant Conjecture. ## F204. Tensor saturation for even spin groups.

Discipline: Algebra

Proves saturation factor one for $`\mathop{\mathrm{Spin}}\nolimits (2n)`$, <i>n</i> ≥ 2: for three dominant integral weights whose sum lies in the root lattice, an invariant at any common positive integral dilation already gives an invariant at the original weights. This resolves the type-<i>D</i> part of the simply-laced saturation conjecture.

## F205. Saxl’s conjecture and universal tensor squares.

Discipline: Algebra
