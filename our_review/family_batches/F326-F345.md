## F326. The cotype–cotype conjecture under the approximation property.

Discipline: Functional analysis

Resolves the cotype–cotype conjecture for real Banach spaces with the approximation property. Such a nonzero space is <i>K</i>-convex if and only if both it and its dual have finite Rademacher cotype, with possibly different exponents. Equivalently, these cotype assumptions force nontrivial Rademacher type. ([Lean](lean/docs/326.md))

## F327. Markov type characterizes superreflexivity.

Discipline: Functional analysis

Proves that every real Banach space with Markov type <i>p</i> for some <i>p</i> &gt; 1 admits an equivalent uniformly convex norm, answering Naor's renorming question. Together with the known converse, this characterizes superreflexivity by nontrivial Markov type. ([Lean](lean/docs/327.md))

## F328. Nonexpansive fixed points in reflexive Banach spaces.

Discipline: Functional analysis

Resolves Kirk's reflexive-space fixed-point problem: every nonexpansive selfmap of a nonempty closed bounded convex subset of a real reflexive Banach space has a fixed point. The result uses the original norm, without assuming uniform convexity. ([Lean](lean/docs/328.md))

## F329. A counterexample to metric-entropy duality.

Discipline: Functional analysis

Disproves Pietsch's dimension-free duality conjecture for metric entropy. Origin-symmetric convex bodies violate every proposed choice of universal constants in the conjectured comparison between covering numbers and those of the polar bodies, even when the covering body is a cube. ([Lean](lean/docs/329.md))

## F330. A uniformly discrete counterexample to bounded approximation in Lipschitz-free spaces.

Discipline: Functional analysis

Constructs a countable uniformly discrete metric space whose real Lipschitz-free Banach space has the approximation property but not the bounded approximation property, answering Kalton's question negatively. Finite-rank operators approximate the identity on every compact set, but their norms cannot share a finite bound. ([Lean](lean/docs/330.md))

## F332. Metric Markov cotype of ℓ1 and Hilbert-space Lipschitz extension.

Discipline: Functional analysis

Proves that real <i>ℓ</i><sub>1</sub> has metric Markov cotype two, answering Mendel and Naor's question. Consequently, every Lipschitz map from an arbitrary subset of a real Hilbert space into <i>ℓ</i><sub>1</sub> extends to the whole space with a universal multiplicative loss in its Lipschitz constant, resolving Ball's extension problem for this target.

## F334. A smooth surface metric with no local isometric immersion in ℝ3.

## F335. Gromov’s integral scalar-curvature bound for simplicial volume.

Discipline: Differential geometry

Proves $`\int_M(\mathrm{Scal}_g^-)^{n/2}\,dV_g\ge a_n\lVert M\rVert`$ for every closed connected oriented smooth <i>n</i>-manifold, <i>n</i> ≥ 3, and every smooth metric, with $`a_n\gt 0`$ depending only on dimension. Here $`\mathrm{Scal}_g^-=\max\{0,-\mathrm{Scal}_g\}`$ and $`\lVert M\rVert`$ is real simplicial volume. Also proves rational inessentiality under positive scalar curvature, resolving the Gromov–Lawson conjecture; every nonnegative-scalar-curvature metric on a closed aspherical manifold is flat.

## F336. Spectral scalar curvature, Urysohn width, and macroscopic dimension.

Discipline: Differential geometry

Every complete connected smooth boundaryless <i>n</i>-manifold, <i>n</i> ≥ 3, satisfying $`-4\Delta+\mathrm{Scal}\ge1`$ as a quadratic-form inequality admits a continuous map to a simplicial complex of dimension at most $`n-2`$ whose entire fibers have diameter bounded only by <i>n</i> in the original metric. This strengthens Gromov's width conclusion to spectral scalar curvature. Universal covers of closed positive-scalar-curvature manifolds also have continuous macroscopic dimension at most $`n-2`$ for every <i>n</i> ≥ 2.

## F337. Sharp Cartan–Hadamard isoperimetry and rigidity.

Discipline: Differential geometry

Proves generalized Cartan–Hadamard isoperimetry in every dimension: in a complete simply connected manifold with sectional curvature at most <i>κ</i> ≤ 0, every finite-volume finite-perimeter set satisfies the sharp comparison with the equal-volume model ball. Bounded positive-volume equality regions for <i>κ</i> = 0 are Euclidean balls. Also proves sharp Euclidean filling bounds for compactly supported integral <i>n</i>-cycles, <i>n</i> ≥ 2, in arbitrary proper CAT$`(0)`$ spaces. ([Lean](lean/docs/337.md))

## F340. A counterexample to the nearby Lagrangian conjecture.

Discipline: Differential geometry

Disproves the unrestricted nearby Lagrangian conjecture. For some sufficiently large even <i>N</i>, constructs a closed exact embedded Lagrangian in $`T^*(S^9\times S^{N-1})`$ that is diffeomorphic to the base but not Hamiltonian isotopic to its zero section.

## F341. Donaldson's hypersymplectic deformation conjecture.

Discipline: Differential geometry

Proves Donaldson's hypersymplectic deformation conjecture in a cohomology-preserving form. Every positive triple of smooth closed two-forms on a closed connected oriented four-manifold, normalized by $`\int\omega_i\wedge\omega_j=\delta_{ij}`$, deforms through positive closed triples to a hyperkähler triple while preserving all three cohomology classes. Any positive triple can first be normalized by a constant linear change.

## F343. Symplectic ball packing in higher dimensions.

Discipline: Differential geometry

Resolves the Siegel–Yao conjecture for arbitrary capacities in every dimension $`2n\ge6`$. Finitely many closed symplectic balls of capacities $`R_1,\ldots,R_k`$ embed disjointly into an open ball of capacity <i>R</i> exactly when $`\sum_iR_i^n\lt R^n`$ and $`R_i+R_j\lt R`$ for every distinct pair <i>i</i>, <i>j</i>. ## F345. Infinitely many closed geodesics on Riemannian spheres and closed three-manifolds.

Discipline: Differential geometry

Proves that every smooth Riemannian metric on <i>S</i><sup><i>n</i></sup>, <i>n</i> ≥ 2, has infinitely many prime closed geodesics with pairwise distinct images. The same conclusion holds on every closed manifold admitting a finite smooth spherical cover and on every closed three-manifold, without orientability or nondegeneracy restrictions.
