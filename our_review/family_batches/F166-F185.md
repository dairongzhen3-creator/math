## F166. The higher-dimensional Erdős distinct-distances conjecture.

Discipline: Combinatorics

For every fixed <i>d</i> ≥ 3, any <i>n</i> ≥ 2 distinct points in ℝ<sup><i>d</i></sup> determine at least $`c_dn^{2/d}`$ distinct distances, with $`c_d\gt 0`$ depending only on dimension. This matches the integer-grid order and resolves the higher-dimensional Erdős distinct-distances conjecture with a constant-factor bound.

## F167. Planar distinct distances and unit-distance bounds.

Discipline: Combinatorics

Proves the weak pinned Erdős distance conjecture: for every fixed <i>ε</i> &gt; 0, all but $`o(n)`$ points of any <i>n</i>-point planar set determine at least $`n^{1-\varepsilon}`$ distinct nonzero distances. A complementary theorem bounds the number of unit-distance pairs by $`O(n^{4/3-\delta})`$ for an absolute <i>δ</i> &gt; 0. ([Lean](lean/docs/167.md))

## F168. Combinatorial invariance of Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials.

Discipline: Combinatorics

Resolves the full combinatorial invariance conjecture: isomorphic Bruhat intervals in arbitrary Coxeter systems have identical equal-parameter Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials. Thus the abstract order of the interval determines the polynomial, even across different Coxeter systems. ([Lean](lean/docs/168.md))

## F169. Shareshian–Wachs elementary positivity.

Discipline: Combinatorics

Resolves the elementary-positivity part of the Shareshian–Wachs conjecture: the chromatic quasisymmetric function of every natural unit interval graph has elementary-basis coefficients in $`\mathbb N[q]`$. The coefficients count explicitly described permutations, giving a combinatorial explanation of positivity. ([Lean](lean/docs/169.md))

## F170. Sharp logarithmic exponents for off-diagonal Ramsey numbers.

Discipline: Combinatorics

For every fixed integer <i>s</i> ≥ 5, proves $`r(s,t)=t^{s-1}/(\log t)^{s-2+o(1)}`$ as $`t\to\infty`$, determining the logarithmic exponent and matching the classical upper bound at that scale. Here $`r(s,t)`$ is the least number of vertices forcing an <i>s</i>-clique or a <i>t</i>-vertex independent set. ([Lean](lean/docs/170.md))

## F171. The hypercube Ramsey conjecture.

Discipline: Combinatorics

Resolves the Burr–Erdős hypercube Ramsey conjecture: the two-color Ramsey number of the <i>n</i>-dimensional cube is $`\Theta(2^n)`$. Thus every red-blue coloring of a complete graph on a universal constant times the cube's number of vertices contains a monochromatic copy of the cube.

- F171-P01: [The hypercube Ramsey number has linear order](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/adc7f1241b42e322a6451854ab7e4b4c146bf78a/preprints/The-hypercube-Ramsey-number-has-linear-order-September-23-2026/paper.pdf)

## F172. Classification of finite Euclidean Ramsey configurations.

Discipline: Combinatorics

Classifies finite point configurations that occur monochromatically, at their original scale, in every finite coloring of sufficiently high-dimensional Euclidean space. The characterization is an algebraic condition over the coordinate field. It also disproves the Leader–Russell–Walters conjecture that every such configuration is a subset of a finite transitive set. ([Lean](lean/docs/172.md))

## F173. Seymour’s second-neighborhood conjecture.

Discipline: Combinatorics

Proves Seymour's second-neighborhood conjecture: every nonempty finite oriented graph has a vertex with at least as many vertices at directed distance exactly two as at directed distance one. Oriented graphs may be arbitrary apart from the exclusion of loops and oppositely directed edge pairs. ([Lean](lean/docs/173.md))

## F174. Deterministic construction of strong thin spanning trees.

Discipline: Combinatorics

Resolves the strong thin-tree conjecture constructively. Every finite loopless <i>k</i>-edge-connected multigraph on at least two vertices has a spanning tree containing at most a universal $`C/k`$ fraction of the edges of every cut. Such a tree can be found deterministically in polynomial time, even with binary-encoded parallel-edge multiplicities. ([Lean](lean/docs/174.md))

## F175. Talagrand’s expectation thresholds, discrete convexity, and graph decompositions.

Discipline: Combinatorics

Proves that integral and fractional expectation thresholds differ by at most a universal factor, and resolves Talagrand's discrete-convexity conjecture. An application proves the Ascoli–He–Park–Talagrand graph-decomposition conjecture: every graph's edges split into a universally bounded number of fixed pieces, each with containment threshold at most a universal constant times the original graph's integral expectation threshold. The pieces' embeddings need not agree on shared vertices. ([Lean](lean/docs/175.md))

## F176. The second Kahn–Kalai conjecture with an edge-count bound.

Discipline: Combinatorics

Proves the second Kahn–Kalai conjecture: for every finite simple graph <i>H</i> with <i>h</i> ≥ 1 edges and at most <i>n</i> vertices, its appearance threshold in $`G(n,p)`$ is at most $`C p_{\mathrm E}(n,H)(1+\log_2 h)`$, with universal <i>C</i>. Here $`p_{\mathrm E}`$ is the least density at which every subgraph of <i>H</i> has expected copy count at least 1/2. ([Lean](lean/docs/176.md))

## F177. Bounded-degree coboundary expanders.

Discipline: Combinatorics

Constructs arbitrarily large finite <i>d</i>-dimensional simplicial complexes, for every <i>d</i> ≥ 3, with uniformly bounded vertex degrees and uniform 𝔽<sub>2</sub> coboundary expansion in every degree below <i>d</i>. Together with the known graph and two-dimensional cases, this establishes the existence of such expanders in every positive dimension. ([Lean](lean/docs/177.md))

## F178. Deterministic nonbipartite Ramanujan graphs in every fixed degree.

Discipline: Combinatorics

For every fixed <i>d</i> ≥ 3, constructs a simple <i>d</i>-regular nonbipartite Ramanujan graph on every sufficiently large even number <i>n</i> of vertices, with every nonconstant adjacency eigenvalue strictly between $`-2\sqrt{d-1}`$ and $`2\sqrt{d-1}`$. A deterministic algorithm outputs the full adjacency list in polynomial bit time, with exponent depending on <i>d</i>.

## F179. The circulant Hadamard and Barker-sequence conjectures.

Discipline: Combinatorics

Proves that real circulant Hadamard matrices exist exactly in orders 1 and 4, resolving the circulant Hadamard conjecture. Together with classical Barker-sequence results, this shows that binary sequences whose nontrivial aperiodic autocorrelations have magnitude at most 1 exist at lengths <i>n</i> &gt; 1 exactly when $`n\in\{2,3,4,5,7,11,13\}`$. ([Lean](lean/docs/179.md))

## F180. Barnette’s Hamiltonian-cycle conjecture.

Discipline: Combinatorics

Proves that every finite simple cubic bipartite planar 3-vertex-connected graph has a Hamiltonian cycle, resolving Barnette's conjecture. Equivalently, every three-edge path in a finite simple cubic 3-vertex-connected bipartite Pfaffian graph lies in a Hamiltonian cycle. ([Lean](lean/docs/180.md))

Discipline: Combinatorics

Proves that the edges of every finite simple undirected graph on <i>n</i> vertices can be partitioned into at most $`Cn`$ simple cycles and single edges, for an absolute constant <i>C</i>. This resolves the Erdős–Gallai cycle-decomposition conjecture, bounding the number of pieces linearly even for dense graphs. ([Lean](lean/docs/181.md))

## F182. Power savings for intersective polynomial differences and prime arguments.

Discipline: Combinatorics

For every fixed intersective integer polynomial <i>h</i> of degree <i>k</i> ≥ 2 with positive leading coefficient, proves that a subset of $`\{1,\ldots,N\}`$ avoiding nonzero values $`h(1),h(2),\ldots`$ as differences has size $`O_h(N^{1-c_k})`$, with $`c_k\gt 0`$ depending only on degree. Here intersective means having a root modulo every modulus. For prime arguments, a power saving also holds when <i>h</i> has a unit root modulo every modulus, with exponent allowed to depend on <i>h</i>. ([Lean](lean/docs/182.md))

## F183. Power savings for planar halving lines and k-sets.

Discipline: Combinatorics

Improves the planar halving-line bound to $`O(n^{4/3-\varepsilon})`$ for sets with no three collinear and an absolute <i>ε</i> &gt; 0. More generally, an <i>n</i>-point set with no three collinear has $`O(n(k+1)^{1/3-\varepsilon_0})`$ strictly separable <i>k</i>-subsets for $`1\le k\le n/2`$, with an absolute $`\varepsilon_0\gt 0`$. The constants and positive exponents are nonquantitative. ([Lean](lean/docs/183.md))

## F184. Correspondence coloring with a fixed forbidden subgraph.

Discipline: Combinatorics

Proves the Alon–Krivelevich–Sudakov coloring conjecture in correspondence-coloring form: graphs avoiding any fixed subgraph <i>F</i> need $`O_F(\Delta/\log\Delta)`$ colors when their maximum degree Δ is sufficiently large. Also proves the Ajtai–Erdős–Komlós–Szemerédi independence conjecture: for fixed <i>r</i> ≥ 4, every <i>n</i>-vertex <i>K</i><sub><i>r</i></sub>-free graph of average degree <i>d</i> ≥ 2 has an independent set of size $`\Omega_r(n\log d/d)`$. ([Lean](lean/docs/184.md))

## F185. Counterexamples to infinite matroid intersection and packing/covering.

Discipline: Combinatorics

Disproves the unrestricted infinite matroid intersection and packing/covering conjectures in ZFC, using two self-dual partitional matroids on a countably infinite ground set. The same examples answer Joó’s partitional-matroid question negatively. They are neither finitary nor cofinitary, so Nash-Williams’ original finitary conjecture remains outside the result. ([Lean](lean/docs/185.md))
